A workplace post-accident testing checklist

Practical steps for employers coordinating a collection following a workplace incident.

screenings4u Editorial Team · 6 min read · October 8, 2026

When a workplace incident occurs, the first priorities are people’s safety, emergency response, and required incident procedures. If your testing policy or a regulation calls for post-accident testing, clear coordination can help avoid unnecessary delays.

Start with safety and policy

Ensure injured people receive appropriate care and follow established emergency procedures. Next, check the employer’s written policy and any DOT or other program rules to understand when a post-accident test is required. Do not assume every incident triggers a regulated test.

Prepare the details the coordinator will need

Having accurate information ready helps a collection provider assess options and availability.

  • An on-site contact person and a reliable callback number.
  • The incident location and the employee’s current location.
  • The required testing type and program (DOT or non-DOT).
  • Whether a clinic visit or a mobile collection is being requested.
  • Relevant authorization and documentation required by the program.

Do not confuse urgency with guaranteed availability

Mobile collection and after-hours support depend on coverage, staffing, and the location. Ask what can actually be arranged and document the steps taken if service is unavailable. Applicable time windows and documentation requirements depend on the governing program.

Protect privacy throughout the process

Share only the information needed to coordinate the collection. Sensitive medical and testing details should travel through authorized secure channels, not informal email or group messages.

Plan before incidents happen

Employers benefit from clear written contact procedures, after-hours escalation paths, and a known process for identifying DOT vs. non-DOT collections. Review these arrangements periodically.
