What To Expect

Know what your New Entrant Safety Audit is designed to evaluate.

FMCSA describes the Safety Audit as a review of carrier records to verify that basic safety management controls are in place.

Core Review Areas

Depending on the carrier and applicable requirements, a Safety Auditor may review areas such as:

  • Driver qualifications and fitness
  • Driver duty status and hours of service
  • Vehicle maintenance
  • Accident register
  • Controlled substances and alcohol testing
  • Hazardous materials, when applicable

Why Preparation Matters

Strong records make it easier to demonstrate that your basic safety-management controls are operating as required.

  • Identify missing documentation early
  • Resolve inconsistent records
  • Organize testing-program documentation
  • Prepare staff for document requests
  • Keep your compliance file audit-ready
  • Address issues before the review

New Entrant FAQ

Questions about the New Entrant Safety Audit?

Review the basics before you begin preparing your records.

It is an FMCSA safety audit that reviews a new entrant motor carrier's records to verify that basic safety management controls are in place and operating effectively.
FMCSA states that a new entrant is subject to the New Entrant Program for 18 months. During that period, the carrier's safety performance is monitored and a Safety Audit is conducted.
For most new entrant motor carriers, FMCSA states that the Safety Audit is generally conducted within the first 12 months after operations begin.
Potential review areas include driver qualifications and fitness, driver duty status, vehicle maintenance, accident records, controlled substances and alcohol testing, and hazardous materials when applicable.
